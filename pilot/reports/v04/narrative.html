01

A deeper chain, and a correction

The previous run measured real-world fact composition to three hops and reported a collapse at four. Two problems have since surfaced, and both change the numbers.

The chain itself was shallow by construction: it answered with a city, then a letter, then an element, then a number, so each added hop changed what the question was asking. Fixing the answer to a single type and extending backwards removes that limit. The answer is always an atomic number; depth controls only how far back the question starts.

The correction

Few-shot examples were drawn before the target question, independently, from a small pool. The target could therefore appear verbatim in its own few-shot block, with the answer attached — 41% of trials. Every fact-task number published previously is inflated by this, most severely at the depths where the limit actually lives. The generator now draws the target first and rejects any example matching it; leakage is zero. Everything below is from clean runs.

Filler tokens, and how much is enough

Filler was given a budget matched to how many tokens the model spends reasoning aloud at each depth, rather than an arbitrary fixed count.

The effect is largest at three hops — the only depth sitting in the sensitive middle of the curve — and small everywhere past it. At two hops there is no room to improve; from four on, there is little to rescue.

Four hops initially showed nothing at all, which looked like a clean ceiling effect. It was not.

03

The same retrievals, unchained

The previous run had no parallel control on real-world knowledge, so its depth claim rested on synthetic tasks alone. The control asks for the same kind of retrieval, k times, with nothing feeding anything else: given k countries, identify one by a property of its capital. Serial depth stays at two however large k grows.

The first attempt asked which capital comes first alphabetically. It failed, and the failure is worth showing.

Against the serial task at matched work, the contrast is direct: at four real-world retrievals, chaining them yields 0.017 while performing them independently yields 0.450. Depth is the binding constraint, not the amount of retrieval — the same conclusion the synthetic tasks reached, now on ordinary world knowledge.

05

Limits

  • One model. Everything here is qwen3.6-27b. The synthetic tasks were run across three.
  • Small pool. Six chains for the serial task, twenty-four country–capital pairs for the control, so questions repeat across trials.
  • The hops are not homogeneous. One step extracts a word's first letter, a character-level operation that tokenised models find much harder than retrieval. The collapse between two and three hops may partly reflect that step rather than depth alone.
  • The two tasks are matched on retrieval count, not on total difficulty. The parallel control carries a scan-and-test structure the chained task lacks, which is why it sits below the chained task at two retrievals.
  • Chain-of-thought needed a large token budget. At six hops the median reasoning trace is 1345 tokens and a minority run past 12,000; those are scored as failures.
